What runs here

The agents are the Society of LLMs project's own code: four roles (backend, frontend, integration, ship) that add a login feature to a FastAPI app under a DAG runner. Every node's output is a typed note in a versioned KV store; a gate must approve it before downstream nodes can read it; a rejected attempt is fed back as a retry prompt, three times, then the run escalates to a human.

The gates are real: output_schema, files_written, endpoint_present, backend_tests (pytest), uses_endpoint, schema_diff (the cross-vendor contract), merged_tests (pytest on the merged tree), decision_consistency, and the Jev gate — here the deterministic jev_rule stand-in (it says so in every verdict; remote uses TypeSafe's System One through the repository's client when a key is present).

The LLMs are not in the loop. A replay worker does each role's work by script (the same edits and test runs the project's own e2e stub makes) and draws its consumption — seconds on the model tier, tokens, dollars, and whether the attempt comes back in a shape a gate rejects — from the project's 23 recorded runs. A draw is keyed by (seed, run, node, attempt), so every policy faces the same futures.

The world

Time is in society seconds (the recordings' seconds; wall-clock = that ÷ scale). Shared by every run: the cloud model tier (concurrent requests, requests per minute, tokens per minute, priced per token — exceeding it is a 429), the local coder (one resident model: callers queue), the sandbox pool (test runs queue), and one budget bucket per tenant in dollars, refilled per hour; the platform refuses what it cannot pay for. Twelve runs arrive over ~4 minutes to three tenants — the cloud tier saturates, the budget binds.

Four policies, one gateway

off — nobody coordinates. The cloud tier answers 429; the SDK retries twice with backoff and gives up; the executor burns the attempt; three burned attempts escalate the run. Payments are collected at the end of a call: a run can be refused mid-way after its earlier nodes were paid for.

gate — the project's own semaphore generalised: FIFO queueing at the gateway, nothing issued that the headers say would 429, a tenant pauses under a fixed 10 % of its budget. No prediction.

needs — the Reserver. Per role × provider × retry quantiles of duration, tokens and cost, warm-started from the deployment's lineage and learned on the observation stream, give every in-flight run a predicted schedule of its remaining nodes; the schedules sum to a demand forecast whose excess over capacity is the pressure that prices reservations. Leases on the downstream tier under an expected-value gate; shortest-predicted-first on a model queue deeper than its slots, weighted fair queuing otherwise; workflow-level budget pacing: a run is admitted only when the tenant's balance, net of what in-flight runs will still spend, covers its predicted spend-to-completion — finish what you started; a feedback loop corrects the forecast's bias, tightens the floor after any refusal and weights tenants by served share.

oracle — the same rules with the sampled futures read through a guarded peek that raises for anyone else: the clairvoyant bound.
